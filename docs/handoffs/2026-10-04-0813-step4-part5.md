## 6. Decisions needed from Davin, with the options seen

None blocks part 6. Each is a choice I made that is yours.

1. **The `SYN_TABLES_MISSING` check is mine, not in your order.** Without it, `SYN` on before the migration is applied (three are pending and you apply them yourself) loses every cycle's sensor rows until someone notices. With it, the cost is one catalogue query per process after the first success, and a refused SYN write that is easy to see in the log and the job outcome. Alternative: leave it to the runbook (apply the migration first) and delete the check. I recommend keeping it.
2. **Refused readings are counted from the log, for now.** `SYN_READING_REFUSED` is one error line per profile with a fixed prefix, and the job's outcome (Bull keeps the last 100) carries the same list. Part 6's kit can count them from either; a small table would make it a database fact. Your decision 5 of part 4 left it open; this keeps the log as the record, as you approved.
3. **`context_levels` are read from the live table when the job runs** (within the 600-second bound), and the stored bundle freezes them for replay. A later cycle that refits `sr_*` after that does not change a stored cycle, but a job that runs late could read levels one refit newer than the slot saw. The same is true of the channel columns (ADR-020), so I treated it the same way.
4. **The twin is a second copy of 25 rules.** Adding or changing a CHECK now means the migration, the twin and the corpus; three specs fail until all three agree. I think that is the price of option (a); say if you would rather have one source (for example, generate the twin from the migration).
5. **There is no gateway-side switch for SYN.** The flag is the engine's (`worker_config.yaml`, or `SENSOR_WORKER_CONFIG` for a rollback without a deploy). Turning it `off` stops the section and so the writes; nothing in the gateway can write SYN rows without the section.
