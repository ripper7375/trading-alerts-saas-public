### 4b. Mutation check of the new logic

**Method.** A script reads each of the eight new sources with the TypeScript parser and makes one defect at a time: a comparison turned over (`<` to `<=` and `>`, `===` to `!==`), an arithmetic sign, `&&` and `||`, `??`, a removed `!`, a flipped boolean, a number moved by one, a string replaced, a condition forced to true or false, a method swapped for its neighbour (`some`/`every`, `startsWith`/`endsWith`, `indexOf`/`lastIndexOf`, `min`/`max`), and the parts of the regular expressions (a quantifier, an anchor, a class member, an alternative). Each mutant is run in a scratch copy of the tree (eight copies, `node_modules` joined by a junction), never in the repository (each worker compares its copy with the repository when it starts and its restore when it ends), against the suites that cover that file, stopping at the first failure. A mutant is KILLED when a test fails, ALIVE when none does. A mutant that makes the run hang (an infinite loop, caught by the time limit) is counted as killed and listed.

## 5. How it works (for the approval)

**The flow, as part 7 will call it.**

```
model text (placeholders intact)
  -> runChecks: numbers against the dictionary, wording (the locale's list + English, the direction guard)
  -> decideFirst(pass, canRegenerate)
        nothing failed ................................ DELIVER
        something failed, the balance allows .......... REGENERATE once, naming the numbers and the kinds of phrase
        something failed, no regeneration possible .... wording: FIXED_ANSWER (WORDING_FAILED); numbers: FLAG_NUMBERS
  -> (the second text, if any) runChecks again -> decideFinal
        wording failed again .......................... FIXED_ANSWER (WORDING_FAILED), whatever the numbers did
        numbers still unmatched ....................... FLAG_NUMBERS: shown, each as the "not verified" mark of the language
        nothing ....................................... DELIVER
  -> buildChecksGroup: the FIRST pass's counts (extracted = matched + violations; the classes and the categories add up),
     flagged = what is still shown as "not verified", regenerations = 0 or 1, fixed_answer, fixed_reason
```

**The direction guard (decision D12 says language-neutral rules are structural; this one is lexical, so it is written to bite only where a contradiction is certain).** Three tiers of words per side and per language: a `stance` (an instruction to take the side: "go short", "sell the rally"), a `label` (the side as a noun: "a short setup", "a buy signal") and a `view` (the adjective: "bearish"). With a LONG reading the headline may not name the SHORT side in any tier, a rationale line only in stance and label (so "the M15 channel is bearish" is a reason, not a call), an answer only in stance, and the watch and risk lines not at all (they exist to say what would turn the picture). With a SHORT reading the same mirrored. With NEUTRAL or STAND_ASIDE (or the synthesis not running) the headline, a rationale line and an answer may carry no stance of either side. English words are read in every locale too.

**The matching modes.** `word`: bounded by non-letters on both sides. `prefix` (a trailing star in the file): the word may carry any ending (`recommend*` is recommended, recommendation). `substring` (a star at both ends, and the default of Chinese, Japanese, Thai, Korean and Arabic, which have no spaces or attach their articles): anywhere. An exception (`{ "t": "sell", "x": ["sell off"] }`) makes a match inside the longer string not count. Text and phrase go through the same folding, so what the file says is what is looked for.

**The numbers.** The model is told never to write one. A literal that gets through is judged: it exists in the cycle's dictionary within its tolerance, or it is a small count, an ordinal, an identifier or a date (exempt), or it is a violation. The check never changes the reply; it hands part 5 the spans to replace.

## 6. Decisions and readings of mine

D11, D12 and D13 are built as ordered. These are the places where the order, the plan or the documents were silent or could be read two ways. They stand unless you object.

1. **The dictionary holds more than 5.8's list, as match-only entries.** 5.8 lists the day line and the last price, the levels of the envelopes and zones, the distances from the last price to each level and zone, the profile values, the event times and the statistics with their n. The prompt also shows each sensor's `details`, `commentary` and `note`, the synthesis reasons and a zone's other facts in full, so a model that copies a figure from them (101.47 above the upper edge, 73.95 containment, a 0.14 runway ratio) would be called wrong and asked again for a true statement. I added those figures, and the times of the data stamp, as MATCH-ONLY entries (never an id the model may write). They are engine output the prompt already shows; single OHLC bar values stay out, as 5.8 says. `DICTIONARY_LIMITS` bounds them (60 details leaves per sensor, 12 figures per sentence, 600 entries) and removing them is deleting three blocks of `buildValueDictionary`. The statistics "with their n" of 5.8 do not exist in the packet yet (the intake path does not read `state_statistics`), so there is no `n` entry; the figures of a reading's details stand in until a later part wires the statistics reader.
2. **A small number is a percentage or an amount when the language's word for it follows.** D13 exempts "small counts and ordinals not adjacent to `$`, `%` or `:`". In English, German, Spanish and the other languages that write "82 percent", "82 Prozent", "82 por ciento", "yüzde 82", "百分之82", the percent sign is a word and 82 is a whole number of two digits: read literally, D13 would exempt it, and "82 Prozent" (the example of 5.9 itself) would pass the number check. `extract.ts` holds the words for percent and for dollars of the 17 languages and English (`UNIT_WORDS`, tested one by one) and calls such a number a percentage or an amount, so it is judged. The words are code, a short list per language, not part of the reviewed lists.
3. **Tolerance and matching, stated once.** ±$0.05 on prices and dollar distances (an entry's tolerance, inclusive, decided in millionths); percentages, ratios and counts exact (`74%` does not match 73.95); a time to the minute, or to the second when the literal gives seconds, ignoring the date (the model is not asked for one); the sign of a number is ignored; a literal with two readings (`4.377`) matches if either does.
4. **The class of an unmatched literal** (`classOf`) is decided by how it was written and its size, not by the dictionary: a clock is a time, a percent sign or word a percentage, a ratio or `x` a ratio, an amount from 1,000 a price and below a distance, a bare whole number from 1,000 a price and below a count, a bare decimal from 1,000 a price, from 10 a distance and below a ratio. It is a label for the dashboard, not a judgement.
5. **The direction guard is narrower than the plan's wording.** The plan says "a headline or rationale that names the side opposite to the SYN bias is a wording failure". With one list of six words that would flag the commonest legitimate rationale of a counter-trend cycle ("the M15 channel is bearish"). The guard therefore has three tiers per side (stance, label, view) and holds the headline to all three, a rationale line to stance and label, an answer to stance, and the watch and risk lines to nothing; with NEUTRAL, STAND_ASIDE or no synthesis it holds the headline, rationale and answer to no stance of either side. Section 5.
6. **The structural "always" rows (D12).** The invalidation line: `missingInvalidationLines(zones, watch)` names the zones whose `{Zn.invalidation}` no watch line holds, and `checkReport1` returns it as `missingInvalidation`. It costs no model call and no trace field: part 5's composer adds the line itself from the stored zone (the zone's invalidation is an engine fact that §5.7 section 4 shows anyway). The data stamp and the disclaimer are the server's (part 5). A statistic's n: item 1. Stand-aside: the server states the synthesis fact, and the guard stops a reply that takes a side against it. Conditional phrasing: not enforced (D12); part 10's golden evaluation is where it is measured.
7. **One shared regeneration.** `decideFirst(pass, canRegenerate)` returns REGENERATE when anything failed and the quota allows another call. The plan says that when it does not, "regeneration is switched off and the numbers are flagged instead"; I extended it: a wording failure that cannot be regenerated gives the fixed answer at once (a banned phrase is never shown). After the regeneration a wording failure always gives the fixed answer, even if the numbers are now right.
8. **What the trace counts.** `extracted`, `matched`, `violations` and the class counts are the FIRST pass's (they must add up, which `validateReplyTrace` holds); `flagged` is the delivered pass's (0 when a fixed answer replaced the reply); the wording `violations` and `categories` are the first pass's; `regenerations` is 0 or 1; a `fixed_reason` that is not the checks' own (the data stopped, the model was down, the deadline passed) wins when the orchestrator passes it in.
9. **The English list is read for every locale** besides the locale's own, because a model can write "risk-free" inside a German reply; the report says which lists were read. A value that is not one of the 19 locales is read with English only and says so (`unsupportedLocale`).
10. **Folding.** For text and phrases alike: NFKC, invisible and control characters out, one space, lower case (Turkish with its own rule), apostrophes removed (`don't` is `dont`), hyphens and dashes a space (`risk-free` is `risk free`); the Latin-script languages without accents (`recomendación` matches `recomendacion`) with `ß ı ø æ œ đ ł` spelled out; Arabic and Urdu without vowel marks and tatweel and with the alef, yeh, kaf and heh variants joined. Vietnamese keeps its tone marks, Hindi and Thai their vowel signs. A phrase is written once, with the apostrophe or hyphen it has.
11. **Arabic is `substring` by default** (the article and the conjunctions attach to the word: `الضمان`), like Chinese, Japanese, Thai and Korean; every other language is `word`, with a star where an ending may follow. A native reader should look at Arabic and Korean first: they are where a short phrase can hide inside a longer word.
12. **The "not verified" mark is in the phrase files**, one per language (`—, not verified`, `—, no verificado`, `—，未验证`, ...), reviewed with the lists and returned by `notVerifiedMark(locale)`. `contracts/result.ts` says a number that could not be verified "is already shown as the 'not verified' mark of that locale" in the delivered text, so the mark does not go through the UI dictionaries; if you prefer it there (the compulsory-translation test would then demand it in all 19 files) part 5 changes where the string is read from and nothing else.
13. **The tool's three commands.** `parity` prints the table of phrases per language and concept and fails when a concept is missing or thin (fewer than two phrases, unless the one is a stem with a star); `glossary` compares the lists with the intake glossary (`config/intake/glossary.v1.json`): the glossary's word for buy must be in the language's long-side direction phrases and its word for sell in the short-side ones, for all 19 locales, and the lists record the glossary version they were written against (`1.0.0-draft`; a new glossary version fails `check` until the lists are read again); `check` runs both, the structure of every file, the freshness of the review sheet and the approval. A Jest test holds the tool's folding and structure check to the code's.
14. **`wording-tool.js`, not `wording_tool.py`.** The plan named a Python tool; the order names the JS one, and `python` is a dead alias on this machine (the reason `prompts-tool.js` is Node too).
15. **Phrases I left out on purpose:** words that are also common benign words (Italian `quota` = a level, French `cote`, Thai `รีบ` alone because it starts `รีบาวด์` "rebound", Turkish `ayı` which is also "of the month", Arabic `أسرع` "fastest"), the apostrophe `´` and the soft hyphen (dead code once folded), and generic hedges ("perhaps", "may"): a hedge is conditional phrasing, which 5.9 asks for.

## 8. Problems and surprises

1. **The Write and Edit tools of this environment turn a single `\uXXXX` in a file's content into the character.** I found it when `ARABIC_MARKS` and `DASHES` of `wording-lists.ts` showed up as raw Arabic vowel marks and a soft hyphen (invisible, and a soft hyphen in a dash class would have been a dead entry anyway). The escapes were rebuilt with a script that writes the backslash with `String.fromCharCode(92)`, and a standing fact is in `environment-gotchas.md`. The same goes for the Bash tool's collapse of `\\` (already noted there).
2. **My first corpus run found one real miss, and my own validator found eleven duplicates in the drafts.** The Turkish sentence "olasılığı" (the probability, with the k softened to ğ) was not caught by `olasılık*`; `olasılığ*` was added. `loadWording` refused the first draft of the lists at once, naming eleven phrases that repeat after folding (`apresse se` and `apresse-se`, `ادخل شراءً` and `ادخل شراء`, `稳赚` in two concepts, ...) and two single-word traps (`بلش`); they were removed. That is what the validator is for.
3. **The first mutation runs found test gaps, not code defects** (section 4b): tables of code points and unit words whose entries no test named, a test that took its expectation from the data being mutated (`words.loose`), digit bounds that only six of ten digits reached, regex anchors and quantifiers of the grammar that no test pinned. They became tests. They also found dead code, removed: the unit cache and the regex-escape helper in `extract.ts`, three comma entries of the sign table (once the comma maps to nothing, it never joined two numbers), the acute accent `´` in the apostrophe class (NFKC rewrites it first) and the soft hyphen in the dash class (removed as a format character first).
4. **The first mutation harness was slow for a reason the repository already records.** Jest crawled the junctioned `node_modules` and `next/jest` loaded its config each time, about 12 s a mutant; a scratch config with `roots` and `testMatch`, and a test sequencer that runs the cheap suites first, brought it to about 4 s (standing facts in `environment-gotchas.md`). I stopped and restarted the run twice; the files of the main tree were never mutated (every run was in copies, and each worker checks its copy against the repository at the start and its restore at the end).
5. **One of my own checks was wrong in the tests, not the code, four times** (a `4.383` that is within $0.05 of 4.377, the order of the duplicate message, a golden dictionary that contains `5` as the minimum stop, the depth at which a details leaf is no longer read); the code was right each time and the test was corrected.
6. **Holding the tool and the code to one table of damage found two crashes in the tool.** The ways a set of files can be damaged are one table (`helpers/damage-cases.ts`: 21 language-file cases, 18 phrase cases, 18 `categories.json` cases) that both `wording-lists.test.ts` (the code's loader) and `wording-tool.test.ts` (the tool's structure check) run. The tool threw a `TypeError` on a `categories.json` whose `categories` or `languages` is not a list, and on a language file that is missing or not an object, instead of naming the problem; it now says it, in the code's words. The finer rules of `categories.json` (a gloss on every concept, the locales of a language, the `modes`) stay with the code's loader alone: the tool reads the file for the review sheet and the parity table, and the test records, case by case, what the tool does and does not police.

## 9. What I did not verify

- **Any real model's reply.** Both corpora are my own writing and my own phrase lists. They show that the mechanism holds and that the lists catch the usual way to say each thing; they do not show that a real model's replies pass, or that the real false-positive rate is zero. The first live run (S2 of the runbook) is where that is measured; the checks report the class of every number flag and the category and concept of every wording flag, so a pattern is visible at once.
- **Whether any of the 17 lists is right.** No native reader and no counsel has read a word (architecture Appendix D). The risk is two-sided: a phrase missing from a language lets a banned claim through, a phrase that is also a harmless word costs a second call and then the fixed answer. The review sheet is built to be read language by language; Arabic, Korean, Thai and Turkish (languages where a short phrase can hide in a longer word) first.
- **Number words are not numbers.** "Four thousand three hundred", "四千三百", "cuatro mil" are not found: the check reads digits. The prompt tells the model to write ids, and a number written in words that is wrong would pass.
- **Negations and quotations.** "This is not guaranteed" and "what does risk-free mean?" contain the banned phrase and are flagged, because there is no language-neutral way to tell a claim from a mention. An EXPLAIN answer that defines a banned word would be sent back and then replaced by the fixed answer; whether part 5 or part 10 should allow a quotation is open.
- **Clocks and dates.** `9:30 PM` is read as 09:30. A year written alone (`2026`) is a number of four digits and is judged (only a date, `2026-09-18`, `2026年`, is left out). The Thai Buddhist year, Roman numerals and fractions (`½`, `1/2`) are not read as numbers.
- **The statistics with their n of 5.8.** There are none in the packet yet, so nothing enforces "a statistic only with its n"; item 1 of section 6.
- **A packet with different shapes.** The dictionary is built from the 16 golden cycles and a few damaged copies; a sensor whose envelope is shaped differently (a level with no price, a name that does not fit the id grammar, a `details` of another depth) is handled as the prompt handles it (left out, or match-only without an id), by tests on damaged copies, not on a live one.
- **`npm run build`** was not run (nothing imports the new code but its own tests; no route, no page).
- **The Python-backed parity cases of part 1** (6) still skip in a targeted run (`python` is a dead alias here); nothing in this part needs Python.
