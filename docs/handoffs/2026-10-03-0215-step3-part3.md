### What the database source does, in the order it does it

1. Refuses a symbol other than `XAUUSD` and a slot that is not a multiple of 300 (caller bugs: it throws).
2. One query for the **READY** `market_cycles` rows at the slot and at the slot where M15 was last collected (`state: 'READY'`, `slot in (...)`). The row at the slot missing: `NOT_READY / CYCLE_NOT_READY` (the cycle-ready job is announced just before the row commits, so part 4 retries). The row untrustworthy (`invalidCycleRow` of the read side: no ready time, a status other than FRESH or DELAYED, bad attempts) or its tuning unreadable: `NOT_READY / INVALID_CYCLE_ROW`. The M15 row missing or untrustworthy: **M15 is left out** (no bars, no statistics), noted and logged; everything that reads M15 answers STALE, as `CycleReaderService` does.
3. `ActiveIndicatorService.resolveAll(slot)` once; `fractal_edt` becomes the kit's `fractal`; a timeframe with no setting is absent (INVALID + NO_SETTING in the evaluators).
4. Statistics: `findMany` pinned to symbol, timeframe and **one** `captured_at` (the slot the timeframe was last collected at), ordered by source, never by time. A timeframe with no row is absent, not empty. The 17 dropped fields leave.
5. Bars: asks for the channel's length of the active row (`largest of containment_n, visual_window_bars, window_bars`, capped at 3,000; one day when there is none), at or before `lastClosedBarOpen`, newest first, then checks every bar again with `isClosedBar`. Fewer bars in the table are handed over as they are.
6. Trims the bars to what the evaluators read: the last closed bar of each timeframe all 33 columns, every other bar the open time, the close and the active indicator's four channel columns.
7. Merges the tuning into the per-source maps and refuses a cycle only when both timeframes USE the same source under two tunings; a refused bundle has `data_status = 'INPUTS_REFUSED'`, which the kit's cycle check turns into INVALID + SANITY_FAILED for every MCD (`preflight.py`: "a provider bug").

1. **The bundle carries the columns the evaluators read, not all 33 on every bar.** The plan's S2 intended this ("the loader selects only the columns the evaluators read"), part 1 offered it ("only the active candidates plus the populated ones"), and you approved a footprint of 1.1 to 1.7 GB for 90 days of bundles, which I measured on replicas where the other candidates are mostly empty. In production all eight candidates run on both charts, so 33 columns on every bar would be mostly numbers. I first built that and then measured (section 7, item 4): the evaluators read nothing else (12 of 12 envelopes unchanged), and the all-columns form could be **several times larger** than the approved figure in production (a synthetic upper bound of about 5.7 times, not a measurement). The built form: the last closed bar of each timeframe carries every candidate (the tier-1 cross-check), every other bar the open time, the close and the active indicator's channel. Options: **keep (recommended)**; or all 33 on every bar (a one-line change, `toBar(row, BAR_COLUMNS)`; only worth it if MCD4 to MCD15 are expected to read other candidates over a window, and they could instead declare their columns when they are built). A new MCD that needs more columns changes `windowBarColumns` and its tripwire test says so.
2. **The M15 supply covers the M5 channel in time (`closed-channel.ts`).** MCD3 needs a closed M15 bar at or before the first bar of its M5 window or it answers INVALID + INSUFFICIENT_BARS; with the plan's formula alone, a short M15 channel next to a long M5 one would have made the loader the cause of that INVALID. The rule: the M15 count is the larger of its own channel and `ceil(T_M5 / 3) + 2`. It adds bars only in that case, and it is not window arithmetic (nothing is subtracted or sliced). Options: **keep (recommended)**, or drop it and accept the INVALID in that case.
3. **For part 4: who writes the bundle text that is stored.** `market_cycle_inputs.bundle_gz` is to hold the bundle's canonical text and `inputs_sha256` its hash (part 2's mapping says `bundle_canonical_json(inputs)`), but the CLI result carries only the hash, and JavaScript and Python write floats differently (section 7, item 3), so a TypeScript re-serialisation would hash differently from the runner's as soon as one number is small, large or integral. Options: **(a) the CLI also returns `bundle_canonical_json` (recommended)**: an additive field of `mcd-cycle-result/1` in `mcd_worker/cli.py` and `cycle_runner.py`, two part 1 files with their tests, done as the first step of part 4; (b) part 4 stores its own TypeScript text and its own hash, and `mcd_outputs.inputs_sha256` becomes the TypeScript hash (two hashes in the system, no kit change); (c) store the TypeScript text and keep the runner's hash (a replay would not find its own hash again). I did not touch part 1's files without your say.
4. **Confirm Q13 as built.** Refusal needs both timeframes to USE the same source under two tunings; the same source merely recorded on both charts under two hashes is the normal case in production and in the replicas (`sr_levels` in v3 and v4), and takes the value of the timeframe that uses it. A source nobody uses and the charts disagree on is left out of `config_hash` and noted (no evaluator reads it; neither chart's hash is right for the other). A future MCD that reads `sr_levels` or `sr2_levels` per timeframe needs the kit's hashes keyed by timeframe (a MINOR kit change). The refusal itself is a bundle whose `data_status` is not one of the kit's four, which the kit already turns into INVALID + SANITY_FAILED for every MCD ("a provider bug"). Alternative: a typed refusal and part 4 building the INVALID envelopes itself, which needs a kit entry point. Recommend as built.
5. **Optional hardening, not built: compare the bars with `closed_bars_digest`.** The loader reads in several queries, not one snapshot; a push that upserts rows in the middle of a read could give a bundle whose M5 and M15 bars come from different versions (the next cycle's push starts about five minutes after the slot, the worker reads within seconds of the cycle going ready, so the window is small). `market_cycles.closed_bars_digest` is the hash of the OHLC spine the cycle was declared ready on; the worker could re-read that spine with the existing `loadClosedSpine` and compare, which also catches a refit of a closed bar. It costs one more query per cycle. Options: part 4 adds it (recommended if the push worker proves slow in B2); or wrap the loader's queries in one repeatable-read transaction; or neither until the live measurement says it matters.
6. **M15 not READY.** When the cycle that collected M15 (an earlier slot) is missing or untrustworthy, the loader leaves M15 out and the M15 readers answer STALE, instead of waiting. That is what `CycleReaderService` answers for the same case. Recommend as built.

## 7. Problems and surprises the next session must know

1. **`python -m mcd_worker.cli` runs nothing with the committed config.** `worker_config.yaml` has every flag `off`, so the result has `order: []` and `results: []`. My first acceptance run showed exactly that. The specs pass `--config` with an all-`shadow` file written outside the repo (`test/helpers/kit-runner.ts`), which is how the stored `.cycle.json` were made. Part 4 must decide where the live flags come from (part 1, decision 5: the committed file, with an optional override).
2. **The same source is recorded under two hashes in real data.** In the v3 and v4 replicas `sr_levels` is `23691bbc…` on M5 and `c6f0b7f9…` on M15 (and in `market_cycles` the tuning is keyed by timeframe first for that reason). The stored bundles hold one hash per source (the kit's provider kept whichever row it read last), the loader holds none for such a source, so the loader's bundle differs from the stored one in that key. The acceptance test makes this exact: `loaderForm(stored)`, derived independently, hashes to the same `inputs_sha256` as the database bundle (`sensors-inputs.pg.spec.ts`, "the bundle is the stored one to the byte of the runner's own canonical text, but for the two documented differences"). The two documented differences are this and the trimmed bars.
3. **JavaScript and Python do not write a float the same way** (`0.00001` against `1e-05`, `4005` against `4005.0`, `1.2345678901234568e+20` against `123456789012345680000`). Measured: sending a bundle from JavaScript is safe, because the runner hashes its own re-serialisation of the parsed values (the v1 cycle gave the stored `inputs_sha256` exactly after the database round trip); the TypeScript canonical text of the three stored bundles also hashes equal to the runner's, because none of them holds such a number. Live data may. See decision 3.
4. **Bundle size, measured and estimated.** On the stored replicas the bundle as built is 0.17 to 0.24 MB of JSON and 32 to 49 KB gzipped (the last closed bar's 33 columns, the rest of the bars 6), against 0.77 to 1.17 MB and 41 to 66 KB with 33 columns on every bar. Those replicas hold few non-active candidates; with every candidate populated (**a synthetic fill of perturbed copies, an estimate, not a measurement**) the all-columns form is 186 to 278 KB gzipped, the built form unchanged at 32 to 49 KB. The count of bars also grows with the whole closed channel: v4's M15 channel is 2,035 rows where the fixture holds 378 bars, so a live bundle at the replicas' channel lengths is about 3,200 bars, which I estimate at about 100 to 110 KB gzipped (32 bytes a bar), or **about 2.7 GB for 90 days at 288 cycles a day**, against your approved 1.1 to 1.7 GB. That figure is arithmetic on fixtures, not a measurement of a live cycle. 5. **`window_bars` is not the channel length.** In the replicas `containment_n` equals `visual_window_bars` (755 on v1's M5) and `window_bars` is smaller (545). The evaluators take the first of the three that is a number; the loader asks for the largest of the three, which is the same number here and always at least enough.
6. **The stored bundles' number of closed bars is `T_EDT - 1`** (754 on v1's M5): the channel's last row is the still-open bar. The loader asks for `T_EDT` (755) and the table gives up to that; the oldest of them carries no band, and no evaluator reads it (the ADR-083 scenarios shorten the channel to exercise exactly this).

8. **The `cycle-ready` job and the READY row.** `NOT_READY / CYCLE_NOT_READY` is expected once in a while (the job is announced just before the row commits, part 4 of step 2). `INVALID_CYCLE_ROW` is not: it will not mend by waiting, so part 4 should write STALE readings at once rather than retry.
