Selecting the "best" test depends primarily on your sample size and whether you suspect a specific pattern of error.

1. Homoscedasticity Tests
These tests check if your model's error variance is constant.

Test	Best Situation	Data Limitation / Weakness

Breusch-Pagan	Large samples with a suspected linear increase/decrease in variance (e.g., as income rises, spending variance rises).	Assumes residuals are normally distributed; if they aren't, the test results are unreliable.

White’s Test	Complex datasets where variance might follow a non-linear pattern (e.g., an "hourglass" shape).	Requires a large sample size because it uses many "degrees of freedom" (it squares every variable).

Goldfeld-Quandt	When you have a clear split or group in your data (e.g., Comparing variance in "Small Firms" vs "Large Firms").	You must manually decide where to "split" the data, which can be arbitrary.

Robust Std Errors	Everyday usage. This isn't a test, but a "fix." Use this when you don't want to worry about homoscedasticity.	It doesn't fix the model's coefficients, only the p-values and confidence intervals.

The Verdict: Use White’s Test if you have plenty of data (N>100). If you have a small dataset and are confident your residuals are normal, use Breusch-Pagan. If you just want reliable p-values despite the variance, use Robust Standard Errors.

2. Normality of Residuals Tests
These check if your residuals follow a bell curve (required for valid p-values in small samples).

Test	Best Situation	Data Limitation / Weakness

Shapiro-Wilk	Small to Medium samples (N <2000). It is the most powerful test for detecting non-normality.	In very large samples, it becomes too sensitive, flagging tiny, irrelevant deviations as "significant."

Jarque-Bera	Large samples (N > 200) and Econometrics. It focuses specifically on Skewness and Kurtosis.	It has low "power" for small datasets; it might tell you data is normal simply because it doesn't have enough points to prove otherwise.

K-S (Lilliefors)	When you want to compare your data to a general distribution shape in very large samples.	Generally considered less powerful than Shapiro-Wilk for regression residuals.


The Verdict: Use Shapiro-Wilk for almost any standard analysis under 2,000 rows. For large-scale financial or economic data, Jarque-Bera is the industry standard.

Summary Recommendation

For Small Data (N < 50): Use Shapiro-Wilk for normality and Breusch-Pagan for homoscedasticity (but rely more on visual plots, as tests lack power here).

For Standard Research (50 < N < 500) : Use Shapiro-Wilk and White's Test.

For Big Data (N > 1000) : Use Jarque-Bera. Don't worry too much about Normality tests here (Central Limit Theorem handles it), but always use Robust Standard Errors for your p-values.
